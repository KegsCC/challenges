## Beginner challenge

Here's a classic. Write a simple calculator program. The user should be able to enter 2 numbers and an operation (+, -, *, /), and get a corresponding answer.

e.g.

```
enter your first number: 7
enter your second number: 3
enter your operation (+, -, *, /): *
7 * 3 = 21
```

You could optionally add validation of user input (e.g. if i enter `"foobar"` for my "first number", that would be invalid).
You could also add support for more operations, e.g. python's `**` operator, or the modulo (`%`) operator.

## Intermediate challenge

Here's a classic, but with a slight twist. [99 bottles of beer](https://rosettacode.org/wiki/99_bottles_of_beer):

Write a program which inputs a value `n`, which **writes to a file `song.txt`** containing the first `n` verses of 
the "99 bottles of beer" song.

If we had a song with `n` = `3`, it would be as so. Note the grammar for "1 bottle of beer" and the final line:

```
3 bottles of beer on the wall
3 bottles of beer
Take one down, pass it around
2 bottles of beer on the wall

2 bottles of beer on the wall
2 bottles of beer
Take one down, pass it around
1 bottle of beer on the wall

1 bottle of beer on the wall
1 bottle of beer
Take one down, pass it around
No more bottles of beer on the wall
```

## Advanced challenge

The advanced challenge is not my own creation, but of the KEGS CS department, but I thought it was too cool to omit:

You are given the file `image.rle` with the following 3 lines of content:

```
12
00:white,01:red,10:brown,11:yellow
3,00,5,01,6,00,9,01,3,00,3,10,2,11,1,10,1,11,4,00,1,10,1,11,1,10,3,11,1,10,3,11,2,00,1,10,1,11,2,10,3,11,1,10,3,11,1,00,2,10,4,11,4,10,4,00,7,11,4,00,2,10,1,01,3,10,5,00,3,10,1,01,2,10,1,01,3,10,1,00,4,10,4,01,4,10,2,11,1,10,1,01,1,11,2,01,1,11,1,01,1,10,5,11,6,01,5,11,8,01,2,11,2,00,3,01,2,00,3,01,3,00,3,10,4,00,3,10,1,00,4,10,4,00,4,10
```

This is a simple [run-length encoded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-length_encoding) custom image format.
Your job is to use python's `turtle` module to draw the image.
The first line of the width of the image. The second line associates codes with colours, e.g. `00` means `white`.
The third line is the main data section. First comes the "run-length" value, then comes the value which is repeated.
For example, the first 2 items on the third line (`3,00`) means `00`, `3` times. And since `00` corresponds to `white`,
this means 3 white pixels. Likewise, the next 2 items (`5,01`) means `01`, `5` times. And so on.
Good luck! You will recognise the image when you see it.
